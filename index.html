Solar Flares

Solar flares are huge explosions of energy that happen on the surface of the Sun. They release intense amounts of radiation, including X-rays and ultraviolet radiation, into space. Solar flares usually happen near sunspots, which are darker and cooler areas on the Sun’s surface. Although they only last for a few minutes or hours, they can release more energy than millions of nuclear bombs.

Solar flares form when the Sun’s powerful magnetic fields become twisted and unstable. These magnetic fields are caused by the movement of hot gases inside the Sun. When the magnetic field suddenly snaps or reconnects, a large amount of stored energy is released. This energy heats the surrounding gases and creates a bright flash called a solar flare.

Solar flares can cause problems for technology on Earth and in space. Strong solar flares can interfere with radio communication, GPS systems and satellites. They can also create powerful solar storms that may affect electricity networks. Astronauts are especially at risk because they are exposed to more radiation outside Earth’s protective atmosphere. However, Earth’s atmosphere and magnetic field protect most people on the ground from the harmful radiation.
